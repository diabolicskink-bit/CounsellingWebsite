# Exceptional claim checks

Use this reference only when the user requests a claim audit or proposed copy
depends on a new consequential external claim that the task and repository do
not already supply.

Do not load it merely because a page mentions counselling, inclusion, a
credential, fee, delivery format, or other ordinary practice detail.

## Start from the available material

Treat owner-supplied wording and information recorded by the repository's
current factual owner as working inputs. Do not independently audit
credentials, memberships, experience, fees, availability, response times, or
delivery arrangements unless the user asks or available sources conflict.

When a needed detail is absent or conflicting, flag it, omit it, or use an
obvious placeholder. Do not fill the gap with plausible wording.

Never invent practice details, credentials, quotations, testimonials,
statistics, reported experiences, or outcomes.

## Check only the material claim

Identify the exact sentence or implication that needs checking. If the task or
repository supplies it, align the wording with that material. If it is a new
externally verifiable claim, use a current primary or authoritative source and
preserve its scope and uncertainty.

Use the deeper check for:

- statistics or attributed external findings;
- legal, regulatory, or clinical assertions;
- comparisons with another service or category;
- testimonials, quotations, or reported experiences; and
- promised outcomes, guarantees, or strong suitability claims.

If suitable support is unavailable, remove or narrow the claim. Do not use
research to generate additional public content.

## Keep the result proportionate

Return only the wording that changed, the material unresolved question, or the
small number of sources needed to evaluate the claim. Do not classify every
sentence, create an evidence ledger, or turn the check into page strategy.

Keep interface wording aligned with actual behaviour. Treat metadata and
structured data as part of the same public claim when they repeat or extend
visible wording.
