# Copy Craft Review

Use this reference after drafting substantial or high-prominence copy, or when
copy remains generic, awkward, over-written, or AI-sounding.

Review the public words in their page context. Diagnose the weak passage and
rewrite it; do not defend it with the brief or describe a large hidden process.

## Usefulness And Specificity

Find the real point of each paragraph. Strengthen or remove a paragraph that
only signals warmth, understanding, inclusivity, expertise, or importance.

Check the opening separately. Give the reader a service, concern, distinction,
or useful idea before practice logistics. Make the heading carry a real subject
rather than a general promise of insight, change, or wholeness.

Run a transfer test: if another counsellor could publish the passage unchanged,
bring forward a relevant fact, judgement, distinction, arrangement, or area of
knowledge that belongs to this practice. Keep plain category information when
plain information is genuinely what the reader needs.

## Natural Meaning

Check what every important sentence literally says and implies. Keep the
strength, scope, and certainty of claims accurate. Prefer a clear subject and
active verb when abstract nouns are hiding the point.

Use concrete language where it helps, but keep genuinely abstract ideas
abstract. Specificity must come from available material, not invented
anecdotes, reader feelings, process details, or professional opinions.

## Professional Register

Aim for the ease and control of a good professional writer. The copy may have
character without mimicking the owner's private writing or manufacturing
quirks.

Use therapeutic theory when it clarifies the work. Do not use it to lend
borrowed depth or seriousness to an otherwise general passage.

Read it aloud. Repair:

- counselling or marketing boilerplate;
- language that is more clinical, intimate, grand, or certain than the subject
  warrants;
- sentences whose polish makes them harder to understand;
- repeated sentence shapes or sing-song cadence; and
- wording that sounds assembled rather than naturally phrased.

## Common Generic Patterns

Treat these as clues, not banned forms:

- opening by imagining what the reader feels;
- generic reassurance in place of useful information;
- therapeutic abstractions without a clear referent;
- ordinary difficulty inflated into a deeper emotional story;
- repeated lists of three or balanced `not this, but that` constructions;
- every fact followed by an explanation and benefit;
- equal-looking paragraphs created from every supplied topic; and
- a closing sentence that merely summarises, reassures, or manufactures hope.

Keep any instance that genuinely serves the passage. Rewrite repeated or empty
uses by stating the useful point directly.

## Structure And Ending

Let importance determine paragraph length, order, and form. Combine repeated
ideas. Use lists for retrieval and prose for explanation or persuasion.

Delete the last sentence of each paragraph as a test. Restore it only when it
adds information, movement, qualification, character, or a useful action.

Finish by reading the revised copy once without notes. Stop when further
changes would be preference rather than a material improvement.
