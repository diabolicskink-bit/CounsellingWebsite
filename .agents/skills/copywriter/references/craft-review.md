# Craft review

Use this reference after a complete draft exists, or when feedback says the
copy is generic, awkward, over-written, lifeless, or AI-sounding. Judge only
the visible passage in its page context. A good explanation cannot rescue weak
copy.

## Does it do a commercial job?

- What can a suitable visitor now recognise, understand, or decide?
- What honest reason does the passage give them to consider Joel, keep reading,
  or take the next step?
- Is that reason visible in the copy, or only in the writer's rationale?
- Could another counsellor publish the passage unchanged? If so, has the draft
  missed a useful practice fact, judgement, or point of view?

A section need not make every part of the sale. It must make one useful part of
it rather than merely state that counselling exists.

## Is there a developed thought?

Describe what the passage says without listing its topics or purpose. If the
answer is only “Joel offers counselling for these things”, choose a stronger
premise or relationship between the material.

Find the sentence with the most value. Build around it. Check whether the
opening reaches that value promptly and whether later sentences deepen it or
only complete a familiar copy shape.

Length is earned when a second paragraph adds consequence, specificity,
contrast, practice value, or a changed understanding. Lists, qualifications,
and generic reassurance do not deepen a thin thought.

## Does it recognise the reader without impersonating them?

Specific client-facing language is allowed. The copy may name common pressures
and consequences, such as anxiety being hard to switch off or a relationship
becoming painful and stuck. It should leave room for variation rather than
claim certainty about this particular reader's feelings, history, or goals.

If avoiding false intimacy has produced “people contact me”, “clients may”, or
another caseload report, change the grammatical centre. Put the concern,
situation, choice, or relevant practice strength in the foreground.

Keep analytical classifications private. A sentence that explains every way a
topic might relate to counselling usually sounds like policy or research, not
like a practitioner speaking to a visitor.

## Do the lines carry their weight?

- Read aloud for phrases Joel would not naturally assemble, overly balanced
  clauses, repeated three-part shapes, and sentences that continue after their
  useful payload.
- Prefer a concrete referent to broad nouns such as `context`, `experience`,
  `support`, `space`, `pattern`, or `process` when the distinction matters.
- Check implication as well as literal accuracy. Do not turn a fact into
  causation, intimacy, expertise, safety, universality, or an outcome.
- Remove transitions, benefits, summaries, and reassurance that merely make
  the passage feel complete.
- Look for a phrase, observation, contrast, or rhythm worth remembering. If
  none exists, strengthen the thought rather than adding decoration.

## Repair in causal order

Repair the premise first, then emphasis, relationship to the reader, structure,
specificity, and individual lines. Preserve a blunt useful sentence over a
smooth empty one. After revision, read only the clean copy once more and stop
when further changes would be preference rather than material improvement.
