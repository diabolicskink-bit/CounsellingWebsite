# Craft review

Use this reference to diagnose substantial copy or a passage that still feels
artificial. Read the whole passage first. Apply the relevant checks; do not
score every sentence or manufacture a fault under each heading.

## Find the problem in the words

**Meaning and usefulness.** Can the reader understand what is being said on a
first reading? Is the useful detail present? Distinguish familiar information
from empty wording. A service fact or common concern can be worth stating even
when many practitioners could say it. If the passage is vague, identify the
missing meaning before adding adjectives or examples.

**Phrasing.** Would these words naturally be used together in polished writing?
Look for strained combinations, abstract nouns doing the work of verbs, vague
referents and elaborate ways of saying something simple. Try expressing the
same meaning directly, then give it the rhythm the passage needs. A simpler
word is an improvement only if it keeps the intended meaning.

**Flow.** Does each sentence follow intelligibly from the last? Look for abrupt
topic changes, unnecessary restatement, overlong clauses and a run of short
declarations that would read better connected. Repetition can hold a subject
steady; replacing every repeated noun with a synonym can make the prose less
clear. Read for the sequence of ideas, not just individually polished lines.

**Proportion.** Does the language give the subject an appropriate degree of
importance and emotion? Notice metaphors that make ordinary choices sound
profound, unsupported therapeutic promises, or a slogan where an explanation
would help. Check whether reassurance answers a concern or simply supplies a
warm ending. Keep emotional recognition when it is specific and warranted.

**Construction.** Is the copy organised around what the reader needs, or around
a framework the writer wanted to complete? Repeated contrasts, exhaustive
categories and symmetrical sections can make plain vocabulary feel artificial.
Keep those forms when they explain a useful distinction. Change them when they
force repetition, padding or an over-neat account of people's lives.

**Fidelity.** Has editing changed a possibility into a certainty, a personal
position into a service promise, or experience into a credential? Check what
was removed as well as what was added. In a meaning-preserving edit, retain the
original scope, qualifications and emphasis. In a freer rewrite, any change in
what the practice claims still needs factual support.

## Worked editorial examples

These are illustrations of revision decisions, not approved public copy or a
voice to imitate. Some starting lines come from the inclusion pages discussed
in the September 2026 skill review; the rest are constructed examples. The
owner identified those pages as still feeling artificial. The specific
diagnoses and revisions below are editorial interpretations, not owner rules.

### Make an awkward sentence idiomatic

Starting line, from the LGBTQIA+ page:

> The work can stay with the conflict, loss or choices involved without treating
> sexuality or gender as the problem.

Possible revision:

> We can talk about the conflict, loss or decisions you're facing without
> treating your sexuality or gender as the problem.

The revision makes "the work" concrete and uses ordinary phrasing. "We" refers
to the practitioner and client working together. The sentence is slightly
longer, but easier to follow. A shorter line such as "We can focus on what
matters to you" would lose the specific concerns and the practitioner's stance.
That may be acceptable in a freer rewrite if the surrounding copy supplies
them, but it is not an equivalent edit.

### Replace abstraction with the actual meaning

Constructed example:

> Counselling provides an opportunity for the exploration of uncertainty
> surrounding a relationship decision.

Possible revision:

> In counselling, you can talk through a relationship decision you're unsure about.

The underlying idea is ordinary and useful. Direct phrasing improves it
without adding a distinctive angle, emotional promise or new practice fact.

### Connect sentences that belong together

Constructed example:

> I work online. I am based in Perth. I see adults across Australia.

Possible revision:

> I'm based in Perth and offer online counselling for adults across Australia.

This connects location, delivery and audience in one readable sentence. It
would suit a practical introduction; it need not become the opening of every
page. A common factual sentence can be finished copy.

### Judge a rhetorical device in context

Starting passage, from the ENM page:

> I do not think either monogamy or non-monogamy is inherently better, and I
> will not steer the work in either direction.
>
> Monogamy is not something to outgrow. Non-monogamy is not a moral failing.

The second pair may be valuable if the task specifically calls for answering
those two prejudices. If the section only needs to establish the practitioner's
position, the first sentence may be enough. Removing the pair is a choice about
content and emphasis, not merely a line edit. Do not replace it automatically
with a different balanced slogan.

### Repair a framework without losing its useful distinction

The LGBTQIA+ headings "It may be the question", "It may be the context" and
"It may barely matter" divide the reader's relationship to a topic into three
neat categories. Before rephrasing those headings, ask what useful information
each section actually contains. In a free rewrite, retain the important facts
and organise them around the service or questions being answered. If the user
has protected the three-part structure, improve the wording within it and
identify the structural concern separately. Synonyms alone will not change
the organisation.

### Leave effective functional copy alone

> Make an enquiry

For a link to an enquiry page, this may already identify the action clearly.
Changing it to "Begin your journey towards change" obscures the action and adds
an emotional promise. The existing label needs no creative treatment merely
because it is familiar.

## Finish the revision

Match the repair to the problem and to the permission to edit. Read the revised
passage continuously, without the notes explaining it. Check for awkwardness
introduced by cuts, lost qualifications, new claims and duplication with the
surrounding page. Keep the version that communicates better. If further edits
only exchange equally good phrasing, stop.
