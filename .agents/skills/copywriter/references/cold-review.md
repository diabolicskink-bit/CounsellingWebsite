# Cold review

Use this reference only after a working draft is complete and frozen. Also use
it to review existing copy or respond to feedback that copy sounds generic,
over-written, AI-generated, or unlike the owner.

Judge the visible artifact in its real context. Put aside the brief, research,
forecast, and writer's explanation so they cannot defend wording the reader
cannot see. Diagnose causes, not merely suspect phrases.

## Create independent findings

Read the artifact once without editing. Then identify only failures that
materially affect understanding, interest, trust, fit, decision, action, voice,
or accuracy.

For each material finding, record:

```text
Observable evidence:
Likely cause:
Reader or artifact effect:
Editorial intervention:
Strength or constraint to preserve:
```

Do not praise fluency, warmth, clarity, or authenticity without identifying
the wording or decision that produces it. Do not cushion a material finding or
invent faults to make the review appear rigorous.

## Run the five core tests

### 1. Source test

- What fact, judgement, process, audience insight, inner state, expertise,
  intimacy, causation, or outcome exceeds the source envelope?
- Has a possibility become a general truth?
- Has polished paraphrase deepened the emotional meaning or strength of a
  claim?
- Does the wording preserve exact approved material and genuine unknowns?

Restore the nearest supported proposition, narrow the implication, or remove
it. Do not solve a source gap with plausible specificity.

### 2. Transferability test

Remove the provider and service nouns. Ask whether a competitor could publish
the complete passage unchanged.

If the answer is yes, determine whether:

- the draft ignored supported material or editorial judgement;
- no real selection, emphasis, or point of view governs it; or
- the source envelope genuinely supports only plain category information.

Repair the first two by returning to the commitment and source. For the third,
keep useful direct copy and expose the input gap. Do not insert anecdotes,
quirks, terminology, opinions, or process claims merely to pass the test.

### 3. Composition-shape test

Ignore vocabulary and inspect the shapes:

- Did each source field become an equal paragraph?
- Are lists of concerns, modalities, qualities, identities, or benefits
  stacked because they were supplied?
- Do paragraphs repeat label -> explanation -> reassurance -> benefit?
- Do unlike ideas receive equal length and identical internal structure?
- Do triads, contrast pairs, fragments, rhetorical questions, or concluding
  morals recur without a content reason?
- Does every paragraph complete itself instead of creating movement?

Restore hierarchy, combine or omit source categories, and let importance
determine length and form. Do not repair repeated reasoning by varying sentence
openings.

### 4. Speaker and artifact test

- Would this actual speaker plausibly assemble the wording on this surface?
- Is a biography behaving like a landing page, an FAQ like an essay, or a form
  label like reassurance?
- Does direct address perform a real job or simulate familiarity?
- Does empathy or authority appear as a surface style rather than supported
  attention or judgement?
- Is polish making the speaker more formal, intimate, certain, or therapeutic
  than the material permits?

Re-establish the artifact relationship and supported speaker material before
editing tone.

### 5. Deletion and ending test

Delete the last sentence of each paragraph and the final paragraph. Restore
only what adds a necessary fact, distinction, development, boundary,
qualification, relationship cue, action, or deliberate cadence.

Attempt deletion wherever two sentences perform the same work. Remove
sentences whose only role is generic transition, announced significance,
benefit conversion, reassurance, summary, or completion. Do not treat brevity
as the only virtue; restore material when deletion damages meaning, useful
nuance, voice, rhythm, or the reason to continue.

## Check the relevant reader range

Encounter only the visible artifact from each materially different situation
already established for the task. Look for changes in relevance,
comprehension, assumptions, terminology, omission, practical usefulness, or
likely interpretation.

Tie each finding to observable wording, order, emphasis, omission, or page
context. Do not invent feelings, objections, or testimony. Do not repair an
adverse reading by explaining what the writer meant.

The reader-range check may add material, but it must also be able to recommend
deletion, compression, or deferral. A draft is not strong merely because it
avoids exclusion or offence.

## Diagnose model-default mechanisms

Use the table as causal prompts, not banned forms. A signal may be the clearest
choice once; the problem is vague, automatic, or repeated use.

| Mechanism | Observable signal | Likely cause | Concrete repair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simulated intimacy | Repeated `you`, predicted private feelings, or unearned reassurance. | Relationship is replacing information. | State the actual subject, arrangement, question, choice, or boundary without narrating the reader's interior. |
| Imagined-reader opening | `you may be`, `perhaps`, or a rhetorical question invents relevance. | The model is manufacturing a hook. | Open with the supported subject, situation, answer, or tension. |
| Stacked taxonomy | Concerns, modalities, identities, credentials, or qualities appear as successive lists. | Source categories controlled the composition. | Keep terms needed for retrieval; organise around the one decision-changing distinction and compress or omit the rest. |
| Over-completion | Every fact gains explanation, benefit, reassurance, and conclusion. | Fluency is filling the shape after information is complete. | Stop at the last sentence with a specific function. |
| Significance announcement | `what matters`, `important because`, or `that distinction matters`. | The copy tells the reader to value a point before giving its consequence. | Make the supported consequence the main clause. |
| Interpretive bridge | Repeated `this means`, `in practice`, `in other words`, or `at its core`. | A statement is being completed with a smoother paraphrase. | Start with the meaning, merge the sentences, or retain the bridge only for a real register change. |
| Automatic benefit conversion | Facts repeatedly end with `so you can`, `allowing`, or `making it easier`. | Every detail is being converted into a positive outcome. | Delete the tail; restore only a supported consequence that changes a real decision. |
| Counselling abstraction | `pattern`, `dynamic`, `space`, `support`, `process`, or `experience` lacks a referent. | Professional texture is concealing a thin proposition. | Name what repeats, happens, conflicts, changes, is chosen, or is available; otherwise remove the claim. |
| Semantic inflation | Ordinary difficulty or uncertainty becomes loss of identity, safety, meaning, hope, or another deeper state. | Resonance is being manufactured. | Restore the nearest ordinary supported description. |
| Smoothness tax | Useful bluntness becomes transitions, balanced clauses, and polished summaries. | Surface finish displaced local judgement. | Restore the clearest proposition and allow asymmetry. |
| Symmetrical sections | Every heading receives equal paragraphs, lists, and a reassuring close. | Generation rhythm is controlling emphasis. | Let importance determine length, form, and whether a section exists. |
| Triad compulsion | Three adjectives, clauses, examples, or benefits recur. | Familiar rhythm is dictating the amount of material. | Use the number and shape the thought needs. |
| Contrast machine | Repeated `not X, but Y`, `more than`, or `isn't just`. | Balanced correction is substituting for a direct proposition. | State the positive proposition unless the contrast corrects a live misconception. |
| Generic empathy | The provider narrates struggle or announces understanding. | Emotional register is substituting for attention. | Supply the supported information, conduct, choice, question, boundary, or omit the sentence. |
| Self-awarded quality | `safe`, `inclusive`, `expert`, `personalised`, or `trusted` carries credibility. | An adjective is standing in for evidence. | State supported conduct, language, arrangement, limit, or choice, or remove the claim. |
| Inclusive sweep | `whether X, Y, or Z`, `wherever`, or `whatever brings you`. | Breadth is performing welcome while delaying the point. | Name a useful scope only when it changes eligibility, relevance, or choice. |
| Tidy emotional ending | The close praises action, promises hope, reassures, or summarises a lesson. | The model is completing an emotional arc rather than the task. | End on the last useful fact, boundary, question, action, or nothing. |

An em dash, semicolon, formal sentence, list of three, or word such as
`support` is not evidence of generation. Judge the idea, composition,
relationship, and repeated mechanism.

## Repair in causal order

Repair:

1. source envelope and unsupported meaning;
2. reader scope, page position, and communication job;
3. editorial commitment, central message, and omission;
4. artifact register and relationship to the reader;
5. structure, hierarchy, movement, proportion, and stopping point;
6. specificity and speaker fit;
7. line construction, syntax, cadence, words, and punctuation;
8. remaining model-default glue and surface repetition; and
9. publication constraints: claims, inclusion, accessibility, search, and
   action accuracy.

Correct a consequential factual or functional error when found, but do not use
compliance checks as the organising logic for the rewrite. Do not polish a
composition that still has a source, message, register, or structure failure.

## Respond to AI-sounding feedback

Treat the feedback as a request to locate an observable quality problem:

1. quote only enough copy to locate it;
2. identify the compositional or editorial mechanism and its effect;
3. return to the last useful source, commitment, or voice decision;
4. revise the smallest causal span;
5. reread the surrounding composition; and
6. rebuild the direction only when the direction itself failed.

Do not run an AI detector, ban isolated punctuation or vocabulary, inject
slang or errors, or repeatedly replace the entire draft without naming the
cause.

## Close and report the loop

Rewrite every span affected by accepted findings. Then read the revision cold
and confirm:

- each accepted finding was resolved in the copy;
- the source envelope and necessary limits remain intact;
- useful strengths survived;
- the reader-range and artifact relationship improved or remained sound;
- no new genericity, awkwardness, claim, or page-flow problem appeared; and
- further changes would be preference or churn rather than material repair.

For substantial drafting or rewriting, report one committed working draft,
prioritised findings, accepted revision decisions, clean recommended copy, and
a brief pressure-test. For a narrow revision, report the diagnosis, revised
span, and surrounding-copy spot check. For review-only work, stop after
prioritised findings and interventions unless replacement wording was
requested.
