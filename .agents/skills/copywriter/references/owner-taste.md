# Vive owner direction and taste

Read this reference for every Vive public-copy task. It records current owner
direction, approved wording, and observed failure mechanisms. It is evidence
of taste, not a complete house-voice template. Update it only from explicit
owner feedback or approved public wording.

## Current direction

- Home should speak primarily to client concerns and help a visitor judge fit.
- The website must sell Joel and the practice, not merely describe service
  scope, demonstrate inclusivity, or avoid making a wrong claim.
- Creative editorial judgement matters. Research may suggest content to
  consider when explicitly requested, but it should not determine the prose.
- Therapeutic methodology is supporting material and normally belongs on the
  Working with Joel page rather than driving the first substantial Home
  section.
- `About Vive` is the right theme for the first substantial Home section. It
  should introduce Joel and the practice before developing client concerns; a
  concerns-only heading creates too cold a start.
- The Home offer needs to make Joel Griffiths, anxiety and depression, trauma,
  relationships, and the practice's kink, ENM, and LGBTQIA+ relevance visible.
- Anxiety and depression can sit together as the broad general-counselling
  passage. Trauma deserves its own developed passage, including CPTSD, sexual
  trauma, and the effects of single or repeated traumatic experiences.
- Some development is useful in the first substantial section, but length must
  come from something worth saying rather than category expansion.
- Avoid em dashes in public copy.

These are ingredients and priorities. They are not a prescribed sentence or
one-paragraph-per-topic structure.

## Approved positive evidence

> Known before you arrive. Not learned as you go.

This current Inclusion-hub line is explicitly owner-approved. It makes a
concrete practice difference matter to the visitor, says it with confidence,
and earns memorability through compression and contrast. It does not explain
the whole service or imitate therapeutic warmth.

Treat it as evidence that concise, assertive, idea-led language is welcome when
the claim is earned. Do not turn its two-part contrast into a house formula or
copy its rhythm into unrelated sections. No complete house-voice sample is yet
approved.

The Home Inclusive practice introduction is also owner-approved:

> I bring specialist knowledge of kink and BDSM, ethical non-monogamy and
> polyamory, alongside LGBTQIA+ affirming counselling. I also bring extensive
> lived experience within these communities. I know personally what it is like
> to encounter the subtle reactions and signs of discomfort that can appear
> when talking about these parts of life, even in therapy.

It states professional knowledge and personal experience separately, then
makes the lived-experience claim concrete without making the visitor's response
or history for them.

The Home Kink and BDSM destination copy is owner-approved:

> I bring significant expertise in kink and BDSM, including an understanding
> of why it may matter in a person’s life and the different meanings it can
> carry. You can talk about power, consent, desire and relationships without
> awkwardness or automatic judgement, including when things have gone
> seriously wrong.

It makes the expertise useful by covering meaning, ordinary discussion and
serious harm without treating non-judgement as uncritical affirmation.

## Explicitly rejected mechanisms

### Caseload wrapper

Rejected example: `People contact me about anxiety, depression, trauma and
relationship difficulties.`

It reports on an anonymous group from the provider's side and uses a bland
sentence to carry a coverage list. Substituting `clients come to me`, `I help
people with`, or another provider-summary phrase preserves the same problem.
Change the proposition or grammatical centre.

### Published audience classification

Rejected example: `Any of these may be the reason for counselling, relevant
context, or simply part of your life.`

It turns an internal reader-range framework into polished public copy. The
neat comprehensive triad is the problem even though its distinctions are
defensible. Do not reproduce it through synonyms such as central, peripheral,
background, incidental, ordinary, or unrelated.

### Generic therapeutic completion

Rejected mechanisms include atmospheric openings such as `sometimes it is
clear`, vague unease such as `something is not right`, generic benefit language
such as `gives you room`, and methodology smuggled into Home through abstract
talk of patterns or earlier experiences.

They simulate recognition or therapeutic depth without enough specific
material and tend to arrive in polished, balanced sequences.

## Do not overcorrect

The owner has not rejected direct address, emotional recognition, or naming
what a problem can be like. Home is meant to speak to client problems. The
boundary is false certainty and generic simulated intimacy, not specificity or
human contact.
